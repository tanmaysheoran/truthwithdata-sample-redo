How the calculator works

Two households want the same home. The buyer pays a deposit, stamp duty (or LBTT/LTT), conveyancing, a survey and a mortgage product fee, then pays the mortgage, maintenance, insurance (or a service charge for a flat) and a new product fee every two years. The renter pays rent and invests the deposit and the buyer's upfront costs. Each month, whichever household has the lower housing bill invests the difference, so both have spent exactly the same.

When the buyer sells, they pay an estate agent and solicitor. The break-even year is the first month in which the buyer's net wealth (home value, minus the mortgage still owed, minus selling costs, plus any savings) is at least the renter's.

  • Capital repayments are not a cost: they come back as equity when you sell.
  • Mortgage interest, maintenance, insurance, fees and stamp duty are just as unrecoverable as rent. Only house-price growth offsets them.
  • Investment growth is assumed tax-free (an ISA). Gains on your main home are free of capital gains tax.
  • The rate stays fixed at the level you enter. In reality you re-fix at whatever the market offers.
